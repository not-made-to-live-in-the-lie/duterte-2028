The argument that ended the conversation

"We deserve the leaders we get."
True — but listen to that sentence again, slowly.

Usually, we deserve the leaders governing the nation. Our leaders are byproducts of the social system. The Philippines at one point deserved Duterte. It is the byproduct of choice, plus the social environment.

— A common framing, often pulpit-adjacent
It feels humble. It even sounds biblical.
So why is it dangerous?
Because it stops the conversation right where the deceivers want it to stop — on us, blaming ourselves, never on them.

Yes, the verse is true. But the real question is not did we deserve it? — the question is do we have to live in it? No, we don't. Now ask yourself honestly: who benefits from a narrative that tells us to stop asking questions and quietly accept what happened? The people who built the deception. The people who need us to remain inside the trap.

Often cited ·Hosea 13:11; 1 Samuel 8; Job 34:30 (Douay-Rheims). None of them end the conversation — Scripture's response to "we deserved this" is always repentance and turning, never resignation.

For the friend who says: "But what about the addicts' victims?"

We can share the lament on both sides.
We cannot share the impunity.

What we share
Grief for every innocent life.

The family abused by addiction is innocent. The child harmed by a strung-out neighbor is innocent. The community traumatized by a meth house is innocent. Their grief is real. Their cry for safety is righteous.

We are not minimizing their pain. Solving the drug problem and refusing extrajudicial killings are not opposites. Demanding justice for EJK victims does not mean abandoning communities to addiction. Both can be true at the same time. Both must be.

What we refuse
Forbearance for state-sanctioned murder.

You cannot extend "forbearance" to theft and killing dressed up as policy. That is not reconciliation — that is collaboration. The Bible asks forbearance between equals in disagreement; it never asks it of the powerful crushing the powerless.

We cannot wait for this violence to reach our own families before we wake up. Impunity is the actual enemy here — the power to kill, steal, and walk away unaccountable. That is what we are fighting, not addicts in slums.
