Inheritance —
the plain version, for the Filipino under 30.

No footnotes. Same evidence. Every claim still links to its source. Read this if you want the receipts plainly named.

A note before you read. This discusses homicides, extrajudicial killings, hazing deaths, repression of journalists, and the drug-war legacy. Read at your own pace. Support resources at the end.
01 · You decide this

Your bloc is the decisive one.

Gen Z is ~18.3 million voters — about 29% of the 2025 electorate. With millennials, the two generations are 60–63% of registered voters. Marcos approval among Gen Z is 14%; dissatisfaction 59% (Pulse Asia, March 2026). Your generation already moved the 2025 midterm. You can move 2028.

02 · The receipts across three offices

What she has done with power, when she has had it.

Davao Mayor (2010–2013, 2016–2022): first female and youngest mayor of Davao; high approval ratings. But also: on July 1, 2011 she punched a court sheriff four times in the face during a demolition standoff (Ombudsman reprimanded). Retired police Lt. Arturo Lascañas implicated her in EJKs after she succeeded her father as mayor. ICC named her a secondary respondent in January 2024 in the crimes-against-humanity case.

DepEd Secretary (June 2022 – July 2024): 0% accomplishment on equipment delivery in 2023 — 12,000 teacher laptops, 7,500 staff laptops, e-learning carts, smart TVs — all undelivered. Matatag curriculum rolled out with no teacher training. Feeding program only 48% implemented while children went hungry. Former Usec Gloria Mercado testified that undersecretaries received ~₱50,000 monthly in cash envelopes. Philippines ranked 77th of 81 countries in PISA 2022.

Vice President (2022–present): ₱500M in OVP confidential funds is part of the impeachment articles. She is the first PH official ever impeached twice (May 11, 2026, 257 of 318 House votes). The Senate convened as impeachment court May 18, 2026.

03 · The receipts in confidential funds

Where your tuition actually went.

₱612.5 million at issue across her OVP (₱500M) and DepEd (₱112.5M) tenures. Acknowledgment receipts named ghosts the PSA could not find: "Mary Grace Piattos," "Kokoy Villamin." More than half of 677 alleged recipients had no proof of birth in PSA records. Forged receipts: identical ink, incorrect dates. COA ordered ₱448 million returned (₱375M for 2023 + ₱73M for 2022).

04 · The receipts on her own bank statements

The receipt her checking history left behind.

AMLC flagged ₱6.77 billion in covered + suspicious transactions in the accounts of Vice President Sara Duterte and her husband Manases Carpio between 2006 and 2025 — 313 covered + 17 suspicious in her accounts; 317 + 16 in his. She declared no cash for six consecutive years in her SALN (2019–2024), while AMLC recorded ₱230.87M in transactions in those same years. Her net worth went from ₱7M (2007) to ₱88.4M (2024) — a 12× increase across her public-service career.

And the drug-lord-linked money, verified by AMLC: AMLC confirmed she received ₱14.8 million from Samuel "Sammy" Uy and his associates via check transactions, October 2011 to April 2012. Per the Trillanes affidavit (cross-confirmed by AMLC on 18 of 71 entries): ₱22.32M to Sara across three transactions; ₱181.6M to the wider Duterte family in 2011–2013. Sammy Uy is named in that sworn affidavit as an alleged drug personality; he is a business partner of Michael Yang, Rodrigo Duterte's former economic adviser. Duterte's response: "Every cent of my money came from a legitimate source."

How AMLC data works (so you can answer "but the data is political"): banks are legally required under Republic Act 9160 to file Covered Transaction Reports (any single-day transaction over ₱500,000) and Suspicious Transaction Reports within 5 working days. Banks face criminal penalties for both failure to report AND malicious false reporting. AMLC officials face 3–8 years + disqualification from office if they breach confidentiality or modify records. AMLC is governed by an inter-agency council (BSP, SEC, IC) — no single political principal controls it. For the AMLC compilation to be fake, multiple independent banks would have had to simultaneously fabricate consistent reports, each facing criminal exposure. That is what makes this the hardest-to-fake evidence in the brief.

To hold honestly: AMLC reports transactions — their existence — not legal verdicts. Only a court rules on guilt. We cite AMLC findings as evidence of what is on record.
06 · The bill she wants you to pay in silence

Speaking out, already paid for by named individuals.

227 individuals charged under the Anti-Terror Act since 2020. 34 activists designated "terrorists"; 30 in detention as of 2025. Red-tagging = 78.7% of monitored 2025 midterm campaign violations.

Frenchie Mae Cumpio — the precedent, not a direct charge. Filipino journalist, born January 23, 1999; executive director of Eastern Vista, radio news anchor in Tacloban. Arrested February 2020 under the Rodrigo Duterte administration. Convicted January 21, 2026 of financing terrorism; sentenced to 12 years 5 months to 18 years 8 months. The first journalist in the Philippines convicted of financing terrorism. Sixteen foreign embassies issued statements troubled by the verdict. Sara Duterte has no documented direct role in Cumpio's arrest or prosecution — Cumpio is cited here as the precedent for what the Anti-Terrorism Act of 2020, passed under her father's administration, enables and which a Sara Duterte presidency would inherit.

And the Vice President's own words, in her own video, November 23, 2024: "if I would get killed, you kill BBM, Liza Araneta, and Martin Romualdez. I gave my orders." NBI authenticated the video as unedited and found "reasonable certainty of conviction" for inciting to sedition and grave threats. Three journalists were subpoenaed by NBI Cybercrime after that press conference.

07 · The bill she wants you to pay in heat

The generation most at risk, handed cosmetic policy.

The Philippines ranks 1st in the World Risk Index and 7th in Germanwatch's Climate Risk Index 2026. 27,500 fatalities from 371 extreme weather events (1995–2024); US$35B+ in losses. 26% of Filipino youth name climate as their biggest future problem. Climate anxiety is a documented mental-health crisis in Filipino Gen Z. Sara Duterte's most publicized climate initiative as VP: Pagbabago: A Million Learners and Trees. No published mitigation or adaptation policy framework from her office. Lumad school closures under her father's administration silenced communities defending the forests they live in.

08 · The bill she wants you to pay in work

A labor market already failing you.

Youth unemployment rose to 11.7% by November 2025 — 744,000 unemployed young Filipinos. Underemployment 13.1% (March 2026). 616,000 workers displaced January–November 2025 per DoLE, partly tied to AI/automation. 88% of Gen Z, 89% of millennials cite skills mismatch + unemployment as critical. Highly educated Filipinos are leaving — often into underemployment abroad. Sara Duterte: no published economic plan for youth jobs. Her DepEd record shows non-delivery on basic procurement.

09 · The bill she wants you to pay in absence

OFW separation, drug-war dead, the continuity.

~2.16M Overseas Filipino Workers (April–September 2023); ~10% of GDP from remittances; ~6.3% of households have OFW members. Mental-health crisis documented among children left behind. The drug war under President Rodrigo Duterte killed approximately 30,000 Filipinos, overwhelmingly from poor communities, per HRW and Amnesty. Vice President Sara Duterte has publicly defended the drug-war policies on multiple occasions.

And the debt that follows you into the workforce. When Rodrigo Duterte took office in June 2016, national debt stood at ₱5.948 trillion. When he stepped down June 30, 2022, it was ₱12.791 trillion. The administration added ₱6.843 trillion in new borrowings in six years — exceeding the combined borrowings of all previous Philippine administrations on the modern record. Debt-to-GDP hit 63.5% at end of 2021 — a 17-year high. Drivers per official reporting: pandemic response, "Build, Build, Build," and procurement opacity — the Pharmally scandal (nearly US$175M in overpriced COVID contracts to a firm capitalized at ~US$12,000) is the most-cited single illustration. Sovereign debt does not vanish at the end of a term. It is repaid out of the income tax, VAT, and excise you will pay across your working life.

For OFW families and for families of the EJK dead. Your grief is not a citation. The numbers here exist so no one can say the cost was unknown.
10 · The empty chair, and where she was instead

Her disposition toward accountability, observable now.

She snubbed every House committee on justice hearing on the impeachment complaints. April 14, 2026: in Davao for her mother's birthday. April 22, 2026 hearing on unexplained wealth: vacant seat. May 4, 2026: at The Hague with her father (detained on ICC charges of crimes against humanity). On confidential-fund queries at the OVP budget hearing: "My explanations will be in my own time." Senate trial began May 18, 2026; under Senate Resolution 39, it proceeds even if she does not appear, with a default "not guilty" plea entered for her. This is the disposition she would bring to a presidency.

11 · The system around her, that would govern with her

It is not a choice about Sara Duterte alone.

On May 11, 2026, Tito Sotto was ousted as Senate President; Alan Peter Cayetano (long-time Duterte ally) installed by a 13-senator Duterte-aligned bloc. Cayetano now presides over Sara Duterte's own impeachment court. The week prior, a shootout in the upper house tied to the re-emergence of Sen. Ronald "Bato" dela Rosa, wanted by the ICC. The math: 16 of 24 senators needed to convict; 9 to acquit. The 13-senator bloc has demonstrated it can move as a unit.

The senators who would govern with her include Sen. Bato dela Rosa and Sen. Bong Go — both named by the ICC as co-perpetrators in the drug-war case, and named in the House Quadcom report (June 2025) for state-sanctioned killings, shielding drug traffickers, and enabling POGO crimes. Eight senators refused to sign the Pharmally committee report (Zubiri, Bong Go, Cynthia Villar, Bato, Imee Marcos, Tolentino, Bong Revilla, Pia Cayetano).

And the institutions a Sara Duterte presidency would face are already weaker than the ones her father faced in 2016: the precedent for quo-warranto ouster of a sitting Chief Justice exists (Sereno, May 2018); the SALN-access restrictions under the Duterte-appointed Ombudsman (Martires) are still being reversed; ABS-CBN remains shuttered (denied franchise May 2020); the Anti-Terrorism Act of 2020 remains in force; the Philippines is outside the Rome Statute for any new conduct (effective March 16, 2019). The shielding apparatus does not need to be rebuilt — it would simply be reactivated.
