02 · Methodology · How we built this · What we do not claim · What we return to

Two tracks. Side by side. One finding the cycle did not absorb.

The data, plainly.

  • Track A (AMLC revelations): events drawn from first-tier reporting on AMLC findings, COA disallowances, the Trillanes affidavit, House Justice Committee hearings, and official AMLC statements.
  • Track B (Senate fiasco events): events drawn from first-tier reporting on Senate proceedings, the May 13 gunfire incident, the May 11 leadership coup, and trial-court procedural motions.
  • Every event in both tracks is dated to the day, attributed to ≥1 first-tier outlet, linked to a live source on the References slide.
The libel guard, declared explicitly. This brief does not claim that any senator, the Vice President, her husband Manases Carpio, her counsel, or her communications team deliberately coordinated to distract from AMLC findings or from the SALN-vs-AMLC mismatch specifically. We do not impute motive. We document what happened, when, and what was in the news cycle. You weigh the pattern. Some Track B events have legitimate news value independent of the AMLC trail. We do not contest that. We observe only what they collectively did to the news cycle.

Three named registers the brief uses — declared so you can tell which mode each passage is in.

  • The fog — descriptive register, no motive imputation. The observable cumulative effect on Filipino news cycles between April 22 and May 22: Track A receded as Track B advanced. Used when describing what happened to share-of-voice, with no claim about cause.
  • The smokescreen — the same observable effect, read with the strategic-incentive inference acknowledged as plausible. Used when noting that any party with structural interest in the trial's outcome benefits when the smoking gun is not the headline. We do not say it IS a smokescreen; we say the cycle has produced the same effect a smokescreen would have, and the inference is the reader's to draw.
  • The shielding — the separate structural argument, not about news cycles at all. The May 11 leadership change installed a Duterte-allied presiding officer and confirmed the 13-senator bloc that can deliver an acquittal on the 9-vote threshold. The shielding is the math. It exists whether or not any news cycle is foggy. Slide 7 is its dedicated treatment.
The recurring smoking-gun beat. This brief returns, on every Track B / event slide, to one specific AMLC finding — the smoking gun, for reasons spelled out on Slide 12: the SALN-vs-AMLC mismatch. Six consecutive years (2019–2024) of declared zero cash on hand, against ₱230.87 million in AMLC-recorded transactions in those exact six years. We return to it because: (a) it requires zero priming to understand; (b) it is the AMLC item most resistant to the defense's standing "every cent legitimate" rebuttal; and (c) it is the cleanest single item the prosecution can ask the impeachment court to rule on as a discrete article. The repetition is intentional and grounded in the observable share-of-voice gap between this finding and its public-perception leverage.

Standing methodology guards: AMLC reports a transaction's existence, not its legal interpretation — only a court rules on guilt. This brief cites only authenticated public events and named on-record statements; it does not make psychological claims about any official, advocate, counsel, or PR professional. Sources: Rappler, Inquirer, Al Jazeera, Bloomberg, GMA, Philstar, BusinessWorld, MindaNews, CMFR, Time, Washington Post, CNN, PBS, Reuters; COA, AMLC, PSA, BSP; Wikipedia (chronology only).

The receipts that should have been the news cycle.

  • April 20, 2026 — COA orders Sara Duterte to personally return ₱375M (three cash advances of ₱125M each for 2023 confidential expenses), on top of an earlier ₱73M disallowance — totaling ₱448M ordered returned.[22]
  • April 22, 2026 — House Justice Committee hearing on Sara Duterte's unexplained wealth; her seat vacant for the third time.[53] Trillanes affidavit testimony enters the record: ₱181.6M in checks from Samuel "Sammy" Uy to multiple Duterte-family members, 2011–2013, including ₱22.32M to Sara.[26]
  • April 23, 2026 — AMLC confirms ₱6.77 billion in covered + suspicious transactions across Sara Duterte's and husband Manases Carpio's bank accounts, 2006–2025 (313 covered + 17 suspicious in her accounts; 317 + 16 in his).[23]
  • April 23, 2026 — AMLC confirms ₱14.8M from Samuel "Sammy" Uy and associates received by Sara Duterte via check transactions October 2011–April 2012.[25]
  • April 25, 2026 — Philstar headline: "AMLC records reveal Duterte is billionaire."[80]
  • April 26, 2026 — Philstar: "VP Sara bank transactions may far exceed P6.7 billion."[81]

Per outlet placement and headline dwell time, this was front-page material for roughly four days. The substantive evidentiary case of the impeachment was, briefly, the lead story.

The defense's standing "every cent legitimate" rebuttal[29] does not engage the contradiction. The Track B sequence has filled the news cycle; the smoking gun has not.

Event 1 · May 11, 2026 · The Leadership Coup

Three things. One day. One chamber.

The May 11 sequence, in operational detail:

  • The Sotto ouster mechanics — a 13-10-1 vote declaring all Senate leadership positions vacant. Cayetano elected Senate President; majority and committee leadership reorganized.[63]
  • Cayetano's prior public alignment with the Dutertes — long-time ally; sister Pia Cayetano in the same bloc; the family's political continuity with Duterte priorities documented in years of public votes.[63]
  • Bato dela Rosa's "surfacing" — after weeks of legal absence amid ICC pressure, he appears in the Senate the same day as the coup. Bloomberg and Time framed this prominently.[82]
  • The House 257-318 vote on the same day — the House action that triggered the Senate trial happened the same day the Senate's leadership was inverted. That is on the public record. The brief does not say "therefore coordinated." The brief says "on the same day."[2]

For the media-literate reader: this is the day Track B begins. From here, every subsequent Senate-fiasco event runs against a backdrop of leadership now sympathetic to the defendant.

May 11 was the day the Senate's leadership chair changed hands and the House voted 257-318 to impeach. It was also the day the smoking gun — the six-year SALN-vs-AMLC mismatch — could most plausibly have been featured as the lead story of any major Filipino front page covering the impeachment. Instead, the leadership coup absorbed the headline space. Any party with strategic interest in the trial outcome benefits when the substantive evidentiary anchor of the impeachment is eclipsed by a procedural-political story on the same day.
07 · The Shielding · The third register · Not about news cycles

The fog is what fills the room. The shielding is who counts the votes.

The shielding is a structural fact independent of any news cycle. It would still be there if every Filipino front page led with the smoking gun every day. It is what makes the smokescreen, if a smokescreen is what we're seeing, politically uncostly to whichever party benefits from it.

The math, declared in three numbers.

The 13-senator bloc is already 4 votes above the acquittal threshold and 3 votes below the conviction threshold. The math is on the defense's side before any evidence is heard.

The bloc, named.

  • Sen. Alan Peter Cayetano — long-time Duterte ally; now presiding officer of the impeachment court.[63]
  • Sen. Ronald "Bato" dela Rosa — architect of Oplan Tokhang under President Rodrigo Duterte; ICC-named co-perpetrator in the drug-war case; named in the House Quadcom report (June 2025) for state-sanctioned killings.[65][66] Subject of the May 13 Senate gunfire incident (Slide 8).
  • Sen. Christopher "Bong" Go — long-time Duterte aide; ICC-named co-perpetrator; tied to Pharmally scandal.[65][66]
  • Sen. Pia Cayetano — same family bloc as Alan Peter; refused to sign the Pharmally committee report alongside seven other senators; subject of the May 20 tears-on-the-Senate-floor event (Slide 9).
  • Plus the remaining members of the 13-senator bloc as identified by the May 11 vote tally and corroborated by Rolling Stone PH, Philstar, Politiko, and Time coverage.[63][82]

The procedural levers available to the presiding officer.

Rulings on motions to quash, on admissibility of AMLC exhibits, on cross-examination scope, on the order of articles tried, on whether to consolidate or sever articles, on whether the SALN-mismatch article gets tried first or last. The presiding officer's chair-rulings shape what evidence the public sees and in what order.

Why the shielding makes the fog/smokescreen consequential. If the shielding alone could guarantee acquittal, the news cycle would not matter to the defense. It matters because political cost matters to senators. A senator who casts an acquittal vote against the backdrop of saturating front-page coverage of the smoking gun pays a different cost than a senator who casts the same vote against the backdrop of WhatsApp-receipt theater. The fog/smokescreen lowers the political cost of the shielding's execution. That is the connection the brief asks the reader to hold.
The shielding's primary risk to its own efficacy is if the smoking gun — the six-year SALN-vs-AMLC mismatch — becomes the article on which the trial centers, because that article is the hardest one for any senator on the bloc to publicly explain an acquittal vote on. Any party with strategic interest in the trial's outcome — the Vice President, her husband Manases Carpio, her counsel, her communications team, and any senators in the bloc whose 2028 careers depend on appearing principled — has structural reason to prefer the trial's center of gravity be anywhere except the SALN article. Coverage choices over the next 49 days that direct attention elsewhere reduce the political cost of the shielding's execution.
The converse holds — and it is the only counter-force in the system. If the fog/smokescreen lowers the political cost of an acquittal vote, wider public reach on the smoking gun raises it back. A senator-judge facing an acquittal vote on the SALN article in front of a Filipino public that can recite the contradiction — "six SALNs declared zero cash, ₱230.87M in transactions in those same six years" — pays a different political cost than a senator-judge facing a public that has only heard about WhatsApp receipts. The bloc's 13 votes are fixed. The political cost of casting them is not. Every additional fact-aware Filipino is a marginal increase in the cost of an acquittal vote on the SALN article. Reach is the mechanism. The smoking gun is the payload. Senator-judges embed what their constituents already know — into their procedural rulings, into their public statements, and finally into their votes.

11 · Why this is the evidentiary core · Why AMLC data is hard to fake

The single most reliable class of finding in the entire case file.

Part A — Why the AMLC findings are damning, specifically.

  • It is the candidate's own bank record. Not testimony from one witness; not reconstructed third-party documents. The transactions appear in the books of multiple regulated banks the candidate herself uses.
  • The volume + duration + scale together do the damage. ₱6.77 billion in covered + suspicious transactions across 19 years (2006–2025), across two accounts (Sara + husband Manases Carpio), with 313 + 17 + 317 + 16 = 663 individually flagged transactions in total.[23] A single flagged transaction is statistical noise. 663 is a pattern.
  • The drug-personality connection is inside a broader moral architecture the candidate's family built. AMLC has confirmed ₱14.8M to Sara from Sammy Uy, who is named in Trillanes' sworn affidavit as a drug personality.[25][26] ~30,000 Filipinos (HRW/Amnesty estimates) were killed under the drug war her father presided over, on the premise that drug-linked persons forfeit their right to live.[46] The candidate cannot easily argue both ends of that simultaneously.
  • It is collected under a legal regime that explicitly penalizes the parties on both sides of any falsification — see Part B.

Part B — Why corrupting the data is a problem for fraudsters.

Where the data comes from
RA 9160, as amended.
  • AMLC is the financial intelligence unit, created under Republic Act 9160.[39]
  • Banks file CTRs on single-day transactions > ₱500K, STRs on unusual transactions regardless of amount — within 5 working days.[39]
What happens if banks under-report
Imprisonment + fines.
  • 6 months–1 year + ₱100K–₱500K fine for failure to keep records.[40]
  • Willful blindness can produce additional criminal charges.[40]
What happens if banks falsify
Bilateral skin in the game.
  • 6 months–4 years + ₱100K–₱500K fine for malicious false reporting.[40]
  • A fraud-by-fabrication scheme would require simultaneous cooperation of multiple independent banks — each accepting criminal exposure for no clear benefit.
If AMLC officials alter records
Disqualification from office.
  • 3–8 years + ₱500K–₱1M fine; perpetual or temporary absolute disqualification from office.[40]
  • AMLC governed by inter-agency council (BSP, SEC, IC) — no single political principal controls it.[39]
  • Banks retain own copies under 5-year records-keeping rule; discrepancies independently auditable.
The reliability callout. For the AMLC compilation on Sara Duterte's accounts to be fabricated, multiple independent banks would have had to simultaneously file false reports — each facing its own criminal exposure, with no benefit to any of them. The bilateral criminal architecture is what makes this the most evidentiary class of finding in the entire impeachment case file. And the single item within that record that is hardest to argue against is — for the reasons spelled out on the next slide — the smoking gun: the six SALNs declaring zero cash against ₱230.87M in AMLC-recorded transactions in those same six years.
The reliability of the data is what makes the smoking gun specifically dangerous to the defense. The Vice President, her husband Manases Carpio, and her counsel have ample room to characterize, contextualize, or contest the interpretation of the broader AMLC findings. They have substantially less room with the SALN mismatch, because the SALN is a sworn document under her own signature and the AMLC numbers are produced by a regulated reporting regime they do not control. A communications team facing this fact has structural reason to prefer that the public's attention rest elsewhere.
12 · The Smoking Gun · You already know this fact · What you may not have fully weighted · The public-perception leverage

Of all the AMLC findings, this is the one the defense most needs off your timeline — and the one that moves the ordinary voter fastest.

A note to this slide's audience, directly. Editors, columnists, and reporters who have followed this case already know the SALN-vs-AMLC mismatch — it surfaced in the April 22–23 House hearings and ran in Rappler's data desk piece. The argument of this slide is not "you missed this." It is: this single finding moves public perception faster than any other item in the AMLC record, and the share-of-voice it has received does not match the public-perception leverage it carries. Treat the rest of this slide as a case for prioritization in your July 6 trial-coverage planning.

The specific facts (you already have these).

  • The SALN side. Sara Duterte declared no cash on hand or bank deposits in her SALN for six consecutive years: 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024.[24]
  • The AMLC side, those same years. AMLC recorded ₱230.87 million in transactions in her accounts across those identical six years.[24]
  • The accompanying net-worth growth. Declared net worth grew from ₱7M (2007) to ₱88.4M (2024) — a roughly 12× increase across her public-service career.[24]

What you may not have fully weighted — why this finding has uniquely high public-perception leverage.

It is the only major AMLC finding that needs no priming. Every other item requires the reader to first absorb something. The ₱6.77B figure requires understanding "covered + suspicious transactions." The ₱14.8M Uy payment requires understanding who Sammy Uy is. The SALN mismatch requires none of that. A Filipino voter reading "she said she had no cash but her bank shows ₱230.87M in transactions" understands the contradiction in a single sentence. Zero priming time. Maximum signal.
It is the finding most resistant to the defense's standing rebuttal. "Every cent of my money came from a legitimate source"[29] may eventually account for the ₱14.8M Uy receipts. It cannot account for the SALN declaration itself. A SALN is a sworn declaration of what you owned at a given date. Either the SALN was correct (in which case the AMLC numbers raise serious questions) or the SALN was gravely incomplete (in which case the candidate has independent exposure under R.A. 6713 and R.A. 1379). Both branches are uncomfortable.
It is the finding most likely to drive a procedurally consequential prosecution motion. The trial proper begins July 6. The SALN mismatch is the cleanest item for the prosecution to ask the Senate impeachment court to rule on as a discrete article. Public attention on a procedurally consequential motion shapes how senator-judges weigh political cost. That is mechanical, not conspiratorial.

The share-of-voice observation, made honestly. When you compare Filipino-outlet headline counts and front-page placements in May 2026 between "SALN mismatch" and "Senate gunfire" or "Cayetano tears," the SALN finding has received notably lower share-of-voice. We do not claim any actor tactically suppressed coverage of the SALN finding. We observe that — by the metric of public-perception leverage per article — the SALN finding is being under-utilized in the current cycle relative to other coverage choices. For editors planning July coverage: this is the single item to lead with.

If your readers will see only one chart about Sara Duterte's wealth before the July 6 trial, make it this one: six SALNs declaring zero cash, against ₱230.87 million in AMLC-recorded transactions, in the exact same six years. The 30-second question that follows — "where did the ₱230.87M come from?" — is the one the candidate's defense has not yet answered.
Why reach matters operationally — the pressure mechanism. The 13-senator bloc that delivered the May 11 leadership coup is the same bloc that can deliver acquittal: 9 votes of 24 needed, 13 demonstrated. The math is fixed. The single variable a fact-aware Filipino public can move is the political cost a senator-judge pays for an acquittal vote on the SALN article specifically. Every additional voter who can name the SALN mismatch in a single sentence is a marginal increase in that cost. Senator-judges embed what their constituents already know — into their procedural rulings, into their public statements, and finally into their votes. Wider public reach on the smoking gun is the only force in the system capable of moving the cost calculus the shielding currently keeps cheap. This brief asks every reader who absorbs the SALN finding to forward it — in their own language, in their own networks — until naming the six-year zero-cash declaration against ₱230.87M in transactions becomes baseline literacy for the Filipino voting public ahead of July 6.

Methodology guard: This brief does not claim the defense or the Senate-aligned bloc has tactically suppressed coverage of the SALN finding. It observes that the SALN finding has the highest public-perception leverage per minute of reading time among the AMLC items, and that the share-of-voice this finding has received does not match that leverage. The reader weighs whether the recent news cycle has matched the finding's substantive weight.
