Hindi Na Muli —
The Thousand Cuts.

The plain version. For the voter with twenty minutes. Twenty-five institutions. Six years. One catalog. The 2028 ballot.

Content notice. This brief discusses extrajudicial killings, the repression of journalists, the killing of indigenous leaders, sexual violence in official speech, and the imprisonment of political prisoners on falsified evidence.
For readers with cardiovascular conditions, hypertension, anxiety disorders, PTSD, or who are currently on related medication: reading dense civic-injury catalogs can elevate heart rate, blood pressure, and stress hormones — measurable physiological effects independent of how engaged you feel emotionally. Please pace your reading. Take 10-minute breaks. Drink water. The catalog will still be here when you return.
For readers who have lost a family member, friend, or community member to extrajudicial killing, red-tagging, or political imprisonment — names are said in this brief because no list of these dead can be allowed to flatten into a number. If reading the names of Kian delos Santos, the nine Tumandok leaders, Randall Echanis, Zara Alvarez, the imprisoned Tacloban 5, or others would bring back loss you are not ready to revisit today, close this page. 01 · Methodology + the Carnegie anchor + the Diaz documentary lineage

Twenty-five institutions. One catalog. One thesis.

Every claim is sourced to first-tier reporting (Rappler, Inquirer, Philstar, GMA, Bloomberg, Reuters, Al Jazeera, Time, Washington Post, CNN, BBC), authoritative academic work (Carnegie Endowment, Timberman 2019), official documents (SC rulings, COA, AMLC, UN HRC), and named civil-society monitors (HRW, Amnesty, Karapatan, CMFR, NUJP, FLAG).

The libel guard. This brief does not impute motive to individuals not party to the relevant proceedings. It does not declare guilt where courts have not ruled. It documents events, dates, and visible consequences. Witness recantations are described as recorded; the brief does not editorialize on which testimony is true. "Extrajudicial killing" is reserved for cases so-characterized by UN HRC, HRW, Amnesty, CHR, Karapatan, or named investigative reporting. The reader weighs.

The three registers. Damage (institution reduced by direct administrative action). Capture (institution nominally intact but staffed/directed/constrained by aligned principals). Normalization (lowered floor of public-discourse + impunity that persists across administrations).

The Carnegie anchor. The deck's primary academic frame is "democratic backsliding" as documented by David G. Timberman of the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace in his January 10, 2019 working paper. Timberman invokes Levitsky & Ziblatt's "death by a thousand cuts" methodology — many government efforts to subvert democracy are legal, operating within constitutional bounds while degrading democratic norms incrementally. The Carnegie warning Filipinos under 45 most need to hear: "Most Filipinos under 45 did not experience Marcos abuses, making them less protective of democratic freedoms."

The Ramona Diaz documentary lineage — credit where the title is owed. The subtitle "The Thousand Cuts" draws on two prior usages this brief explicitly acknowledges: (1) the Carnegie/Timberman 2019 framing above; and (2) A Thousand Cuts, the 2020 documentary film by Filipino-American director Ramona S. Diaz about Maria Ressa, Rappler, and the press-freedom struggle under Duterte (Sundance world premiere January 25, 2020). This brief uses the phrase in the same lineage; it does not claim originality on it. Credit to Ramona S. Diaz, David G. Timberman, and Levitsky & Ziblatt.

02 · The judiciary · Sereno + Ombudsman

The chief justice was removed by the body she led. The Ombudsman stopped showing what the powerful own.

Supreme Court · Sereno quo warranto · May 11, 2018. SC voted 8-6 to grant the quo warranto petition filed by Solicitor General Jose Calida, nullifying Chief Justice Maria Lourdes Sereno's appointment over alleged SALN-filing irregularities. First Filipino Chief Justice removed by quo warranto rather than impeachment. The International Commission of Jurists characterized it as contributing to "the deterioration of the rule of law." Justice Samuel Martires voted to oust Sereno; later that year he was appointed Ombudsman. Why this institution: a captured SC is the multiplier that lets every other capture stand.

Office of the Ombudsman · Martires SALN memo · September 1, 2020. Memorandum Circular No. 1 series of 2020 restricted public access to SALNs filed at the Ombudsman — released only with notarized letter of authority, court order, or via Field Investigation Office. PCIJ's July 2021 request for Duterte's SALN hit a dead end. Martires in a September 2021 Senate budget hearing: "No matter how much criticism I will receive, even if I will be bashed, even if I will be removed from office, I will not butt in to that." Restoration announced in 2025 by Ombudsman Remulla. Why this institution: SALN access is the precondition for documenting unexplained wealth. The memo did not require legislation — it required one signature.

03 · The coercive apparatus · PNP + AFP/NTF-ELCAC + ATA + ICC

A 17-year-old. A red-tagging body count. A law that lets the executive name terrorists. A withdrawn ICC membership that did not erase jurisdiction.

PNP · Tokhang · Kian delos Santos. ~6,000 killings per PNP official figure; ~30,000 per HRW/Amnesty. Kian (17, killed Caloocan August 16, 2017): officers Oares, Pereda, Cruz convicted November 2018; SC affirmed August 11, 2025 (announced December 22, 2025). The "ninja cops" scandal: November 2013 Pampanga raid where ₱648M shabu was undeclared; acting Pampanga police chief at the time was Oscar Albayalde, later PNP Chief (2018-2019). PDEA chief Aaron Aquino testified Albayalde had intervened against the dismissal of the 13 "ninja cops." Albayalde resigned October 2019. March 2025: DOJ Secretary Remulla admitted 95% of wrongful deaths under Tokhang had no police reports.

AFP + NTF-ELCAC · red-tagging. EO 70 signed December 4, 2018 created NTF-ELCAC. Karapatan documents 427 activists killed after red-tagging — including peace consultant Randall Echanis (killed in his Quezon City home August 10, 2020) and Karapatan legal worker Zara Alvarez (shot dead in Bacolod City August 17, 2020). May 8, 2024: SC ruled red-tagging threatens "a person's right to life, liberty, or security." September 2023: Ombudsman convicted Parlade and Badoy of "conduct prejudicial." HRW and Karapatan called for NTF-ELCAC's abolition.

Anti-Terrorism Act 2020. Signed July 3, 2020. ATC empowered to designate terrorists administratively. December 9, 2021: SC ruled on 37 consolidated challenges; only two provisions struck down (Section 4 qualifier + Section 25 paragraph 2 foreign-request designation pathway). The bulk of the ATA stands. The Cumpio case (below) is its prototype.

Customs + BuCor · Faeldon. May 26, 2017: ₱6.4B shabu shipment (602.2 kg) at Valenzuela warehouse while Faeldon was BoC Commissioner. He resigned, then was redeployed to BuCor where the 2019 GCTA scandal (near-release of convicted rapist-murderer Antonio Sanchez) ended his tenure there too. Same official, two scandals.

ICC withdrawal. Notice deposited March 17, 2018 (~1 month after the ICC prosecutor's preliminary examination of the drug war was announced); withdrawal effective March 17, 2019. Under Article 127 the ICC retained jurisdiction over crimes committed Nov 1, 2011 – March 16, 2019. September 15, 2021: ICC Pre-Trial Chamber I authorized full investigation. March 2025: Duterte arrested and surrendered to the ICC.

An intact coercive apparatus is the daily institutional layer where state power touches civilian life. When the police institution operates a shoot-to-kill doctrine while its highest officer is alleged to have shielded drug-recycling subordinates, when the military institution operates a red-tagging architecture the SC itself rules threatens the right to life, when a sweeping anti-terror law lets the executive name terrorists administratively, and when the international accountability court is withdrawn from but cannot be escaped — the catalog of damage is structural and the restoration is multi-year.
04 · Watchdog bodies hollowed · CHR + OVP

The body that investigates human-rights abuses got ₱1,000 for the year. The Vice President got nineteen days on drug-war policy.

CHR · ₱1,000 budget · September 12, 2017. House voted 119-32 to allocate CHR a budget of ₱1,000 for FY2018 (motion by SAGIP Rep. Rodante Marcoleta). The Commission had requested ₱623M. 8 days later (September 20, 2017): the House restored the budget to ~₱678M after public outcry — but only after CHR Chair Chito Gascon agreed the Commission would expand its probes to cover non-state actors (criminals, terrorists) victimizing police, military, and civilians, in addition to its drug-war investigations. The restoration came at the cost of a substantively broadened — and arguably diluted — investigative agenda. Why this institution: the chilling effect on CHR's drug-war investigations did not require a single peso of cut. It required the demonstrated willingness to threaten the cut.

Office of the Vice President · Robredo's 19 days at ICAD · November 2019. Robredo accepted appointment as ICAD co-chair November 6, 2019; fired November 24, 2019 — 19 days later. In those 19 days she asked for the list of high-value targets, met with multiple agencies, visited rehab centers, and presented a 40-page report concluding the drug war had reduced shabu supply by ~1% across three years. Amnesty characterization: "Vice President Robredo was never given the chance to stop drug killings." Why this institution: the appoint-then-dismiss mechanism is now in the playbook for procedurally isolating an independently-elected VP.

05 · Pandemic-era capture · DOH/Pharmally + IATF + press blackout

A ₱625,000 paid-up-capital company won ₱8.68 billion in pandemic contracts.

DOH + Pharmally + PS-DBM. Pharmally Pharmaceutical Corporation (paid-up capital ₱625,000) received ₱10.85 billion total contracts (PS-DBM ₱8.68B, DOH, DOTr-PNR) during the pandemic. Michael Yang — Chinese national; Duterte's "Economic Adviser to the President" — introduced Chinese suppliers. Nine contracts signed by Lloyd Christopher Lao (PS-DBM OIC from January 2, 2020). The Senate Blue Ribbon Committee (Chair Sen. Dick Gordon) found a "grand conspiracy" and recommended plunder, graft, and other criminal charges against Health Secretary Francisco Duque III, ex-DBM officials, Pharmally executives, and recommended cases be filed against Duterte after his term for "condoning" the scheme. The Committee found Duterte had "betrayed the public trust." April 2026: CA upheld charges against ex-DBM officials.

IATF + the pandemic press blackout. The Inter-Agency Task Force became the de facto pandemic decision-maker; meeting minutes were not routinely published. PhilHealth fraud surfaced at Senate hearings. ABS-CBN was shut down July 10, 2020 — during the worst public-health emergency of the century — removing the broadcaster whose distribution reach to rural and provincial audiences print and digital outlets did not match. Procurement opacity + closed broadcaster + pandemic restrictions on physical reporting = unprecedented information gap during exactly the period accountability journalism was most needed.

An intact pandemic-response architecture must be subject to extraordinary transparency precisely because its emergency powers are extraordinary. Closed-door inter-agency decisions about community quarantine, vaccine allocation, and contractor selection produce policy with massive distributive consequences. When that architecture operates without routine minutes and without independent audit, the public loses the ability to identify which decisions were epidemiological and which were not.
06 · Local-governance sabotage · DPWH/BBB + LGU acceptance rule

The quality-control safeguard against ghost projects was quietly removed — then restored only after Marcos.

DPWH + Build Build Build. Launched April 18, 2017 as the centerpiece of administration economic policy. By April 28, 2022 — two months before Duterte left office — only 12 of 119 infrastructure flagship projects had been completed. PCIJ September 2018: "Corruption, politics, slippage mar many projects."

The LGU project-acceptance rule that vanished. Longstanding administrative procedure: LGUs must formally accept (sign off on) completed national-government infrastructure projects before turnover — a quality-assurance step preventing ghost projects. Per President Marcos's subsequent statement: "The rule, which President Marcos said was a safeguard to ensure the quality of nationally funded infrastructure projects, was removed by the previous administration." Marcos ordered restoration 2024-2025. The League of Cities backed the directive.

This is the textbook example of Carnegie's "death by a thousand cuts" mechanism: the law did not change; the public was not consulted; the procedural safeguard simply vanished, becoming visible only when the next administration had to explicitly restore it. The restoration is the receipt that the removal happened. The 2028 audit question: which 2017-2022 national projects were turned over without LGU sign-off, and what is their current condition?
07 · Indigenous peoples · Tumandok + Lumad schools + NCIP

Nine indigenous leaders killed in a 4 AM joint police-military operation. 216 Lumad schools' permits revoked. NCIP chronically underfunded.

Tumandok massacre · December 30, 2020. Joint PNP-AFP operation ~4 AM in the villages of Tapaz (Capiz) and Calinog (Iloilo). Nine Tumandok leaders killed, 17 arrested. Their names, said in this brief because no list of these dead can be flattened: Roy Giganto, Reynaldo Katipunan, Galson Catamin, Mario Aguirre, Eliseo Gayas Jr., Maurito Diaz, Artilito Katipunan, Jomar Vidal, Rolando Diaz. Context: they had been organizing against the Jalaur megadam project; the AFP had publicly red-tagged Tumandok members before the operation. December 30, 2021: 15 of 16 detained Tumandok released after the warrants were declared invalid.

Lumad schools. October 2019: DepEd ordered permanent closure of 55 Salugpongan schools over allegations of teaching "left-leaning ideologies." 216 Lumad schools total had their permits revoked across Mindanao under the "Whole-of-Nation" approach. Save Our Schools Network: DepEd "reduced itself as a stamp pad" for the military. ~10,000 students disenfranchised. February 2021: the Bakwit School at UP Cebu was raided.

NCIP. Chronically among the lowest-budget agencies; FY2025 saw an additional ~₱400M cut. NCIP-Davao suspended FY2025 IP scholarships. CADT/CADC processing — the technical work that converts ancestral-domain claims into legally enforceable titles — slowed by the shortfall.

Red-tagging-as-prelude-to-killing is a documented operational pattern. The Tumandok warrants were later found invalid; the dead remain dead. The Lumad-school closures used the same Whole-of-Nation architecture that NTF-ELCAC operationalized nationally — converting basic-education permits into instruments of community-targeted suppression. NCIP underfunding is not a fiscal-discipline decision; it is a structural decision about whose ancestral-domain claims get processed and whose do not.
08 · Press freedom · ABS-CBN + Maria Ressa + Frenchie Mae Cumpio

70 to 11. A Nobel Peace Prize winner convicted. A 22-year-old journalist held 6 years before verdict.

ABS-CBN. July 10, 2020: House Committee voted 70 to deny / 11 to renew / 2 inhibit / 1 abstain on ABS-CBN's franchise — after 12 public hearings (May 26 – July 9) found the broadcaster had not breached any legal provisions. ~11,000 jobs lost during a pandemic. Duterte later admitted using "presidential powers" against the renewal, contradicting earlier statements of neutrality. Amnesty Philippines: "another nail in the coffin of press freedom."

Rappler + Maria Ressa. 2018: SEC ordered Rappler's closure over foreign-ownership allegations. June 15, 2020: Manila RTC convicted Ressa and former Rappler researcher Reynaldo Santos Jr. of cyber libel. October 8, 2021: Ressa awarded Nobel Peace Prize (with Dmitry Muratov). 2024: CA overturned SEC revocation. June 19, 2025: Ressa + 5 Rappler directors acquitted in the anti-dummy case — court found prosecution evidence "grossly insufficient." Cyber-libel conviction still on Supreme Court appeal; Solicitor General has recommended acquittal. The press-freedom struggle of this period was documented in real time by Filipino-American director Ramona S. Diaz in her 2020 feature documentary A Thousand Cuts — the film from which this brief acknowledges its subtitle.

Frenchie Mae Cumpio. Eastern Vista executive director, Aksyon Radyo Tacloban anchor. Arrested in predawn raids February 7, 2020 (Tacloban 5). Initial charge: illegal possession of firearms. October 13, 2021: terrorism-financing charge added under the Anti-Terrorism Act framework. Pretrial detention: ~6 years. 2026: convicted; CPJ in January 2026 condemned the up-to-18-year sentence as "absurd." UN Special Rapporteur Irene Khan: charges appeared to be "in retaliation for her work as a journalist."

09 · Political prisoners + falsified evidence · De Lima + Trillanes

A sitting senator detained 6 years 9 months on testimony five key witnesses recanted. The Supreme Court eventually struck down the unilateral amnesty revocation.

Sen. Leila de Lima · February 2017 – November 2023. Detained after leading the Senate Davao Death Squad investigation (Slide 14 below). Five key prosecution witnesses recanted under oath: Kerwin Espinosa said he had been "coerced, pressured, intimidated, and seriously threatened"; Rafael Ragos (former BuCor head) said he was "forced by government officials" to make his statement; Ronnie Dayan (former bodyguard, co-accused); Rodolfo Magleo; Nonilo Arile. February 2021: first acquittal. May 2023: second acquittal (Judge Alcantara: "Without [the recanted] testimony, the crucial link to establish conspiracy is shrouded with reasonable doubt"). November 13, 2023: final release. Total detention: ~6 years, 9 months. The brief presents the recantations as recorded; it does not editorialize on which testimony was truthful.

Sen. Antonio Trillanes · Proclamation 572 · August 31, 2018. Duterte issued Proclamation 572 declaring Trillanes's 2010 amnesty (granted by President Aquino via Proclamation No. 75) void ab initio; ordered DOJ, AFP, PNP to revive prosecutions. Trial-court split: Branch 148 (Soriano) denied DOJ's bid to revive coup d'etat charges; Branch 150 (Alameda) reinstated the rebellion case. CA later: Branch 150 committed grave abuse of discretion. April 3, 2024: Supreme Court En Banc ruled Proclamation 572 unconstitutional — a President cannot revoke amnesty without congressional concurrence.

10 · Foreign policy + defense · WPS + Russia helicopters

The 2016 UNCLOS ruling Filipinos won — set aside, rhetorically, for six years. ₱12.7 billion in Russian helicopters ordered, then cancelled.

West Philippine Sea. July 12, 2016: Permanent Court of Arbitration ruled in favor of the Philippines under UNCLOS; China's nine-dash-line claim found to have no legal basis. The ruling came down 12 days into Duterte's term. He "set it aside" for the duration of his term in favor of bilateral engagement with China. Operational cost: Chinese maritime militia + coast guard presence in the WPS became routinized; Filipino fishermen displaced from traditional fishing grounds; Reed Bank, Scarborough Shoal, Ayungin Shoal became sites of regular confrontation. 2022 onward: Marcos administration partially reactivated the ruling rhetorically.

AFP modernization Russia pivot. November 2021: Philippines agreed to pay ₱12.7B (~$244M) for 16 Mi-17 helicopters from Russia. Duterte publicly called Putin his "idol." August 10, 2022: Marcos administration cancelled the deal over US CAATSA sanctions exposure following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Defense Secretary Lorenzana: "We could face sanctions." October 2022: US provided $100M in foreign military funding as partial compensation.

11 · Normalization · misogyny + POGOs

Words that became official record. An industry welcomed by executive order; 60+ killings followed.

Misogyny normalized. February 7, 2018, Malacañang speech to former NPA members (per government transcript): "We will just shoot your [genitals], so that if there are no more [genitals], you would be useless." HRW characterization: the remark "encourages state forces to commit sexual violence during armed conflict, which is a violation of international humanitarian law." 1989 Davao mayoralty: said publicly that he "should have been first" to rape an Australian missionary killed in a Davao jail uprising. 2017 Marawi speech to soldiers: told troops they could rape up to three women without punishment. July 2017: rape jokes about Miss Universe contestants. June 2018, Seoul: kissed a Filipina OFW on stage. These are official record; the normalization happens when they stop shocking.

POGOs. 2017: Executive Order 13 institutionalized POGO licensing. Crime profile 2017-2022: human trafficking, kidnapping, robbery-extortion, money laundering, online scams, debt bondage, torture of foreign workers, forced labor. PAOCC: 60-63 POGO-related killings between 2017-2023. March 2024 raid (under Marcos): 868 POGO workers rescued from one Pampanga complex. July 22, 2024: Marcos announced immediate ban; EO 74 implemented November 5, 2024.

12 · DDS confessions · Matobato + Lascañas

Two former insiders. Two Senate confessions. One recanted his denial.

September 2016: Edgar Matobato testified to the Senate (Sen. De Lima presiding) that he had been a member of the Davao Death Squad and that then-Mayor Rodrigo Duterte had commanded the group. October 2016: Arturo Lascañas initially denied DDS membership, refuting Matobato. February 20, 2017: Lascañas reversed his denial and testified the group had operated in Davao from the mid-1990s to as recently as 2013; his earlier denial had been ordered by Duterte's longtime aide SPO4 Sonny Buenaventura, who said his family would be at risk if he testified. Lascañas testified he killed almost 200 people, was paid by Duterte; DDS killed not only criminals but Duterte's political and media enemies. The Philippine government characterized Lascañas's testimony at the time as "fabrication." Philstar concluded the two testimonies "generally match." The DDS-era killings (Nov 2011 onward) sit within the ICC's jurisdictional window — predicate for the ongoing investigation.

14 · The 2028 Watchlist · candidate due-diligence

Twenty-five+ institutions. Fourteen questions. Apply to every candidate.

This is not a partisan endorsement list. It is a due-diligence framework. Apply it to every 2028 presidential candidate. The institutions are bigger than any one candidacy.

  • 1. Did the candidate hold appointed or elected office under Duterte (2016-2022)? In which institution; what role in any of the catalog items above?
  • 2. Has the candidate publicly committed to re-accession to the Rome Statute (ICC)?
  • 3. Has the candidate publicly committed to abolishing NTF-ELCAC (which the SC ruled threatens rights to life, liberty, security)?
  • 4. Has the candidate publicly committed to restoring the full CHR budget request AND statutory protections against budget-coercion?
  • 5. Has the candidate committed to reversing the Ombudsman's SALN-confidentiality framework at a statutory level?
  • 6. What is the candidate's position on the ATA ATC designation authority? What reforms have they proposed?
  • 7. What is the candidate's documented relationship with the Davao oligarchy / Carpio family / Sara Duterte's communications team?
  • 8. Documented record on press freedom — ABS-CBN restoration, Ressa case, Cumpio case?
  • 9. Stated position on the WPS — active enforcement of the 2016 UNCLOS ruling?
  • 10. Documented record on IP policy — NCIP funding, ancestral-domain processing, Lumad-school reopening, Tumandok justice?
  • 11. Position on the DPWH LGU-acceptance rule — will the candidate commit to keeping it restored AND to a public audit of 2017-2022 projects turned over without LGU sign-off?
  • 12. Did the candidate publicly object, in real time, to any of the documented misogyny statements? Their 2018-2022 record answers this.
  • 13. Position on POGOs and the 2024 ban — will the candidate keep POGOs banned?
  • 14. Position on the Trillanes precedent — that no president may revoke amnesty without congressional concurrence?
15 · What this brief is not + the ask

A catalog, not a partisan endorsement. A due-diligence framework, not a prosecution.

Not a partisan endorsement of any 2028 candidate; not a claim that the Duterte presidency was a monolithic conspiracy (the brief documents 25+ separate institutional injuries, each sourced separately — the pattern is the catalog); not a substitute for the reader's own reading of the cited sources; not a declaration of criminal guilt where courts have not ruled. It is a catalog of damage assembled in one place so the reader has the institutional memory the Carnegie report warned Filipinos under 45 would lack.

The ask. For Filipino voters: apply the 14-question Watchlist to every 2028 candidate; talk to one Filipino under 45 about this catalog before the October 2027 Certificate of Candidacy filing window. For Filipino editors, op-ed writers, civic-tech builders: extend the catalog; build the public registry; fact-check candidate statements against this baseline. For international correspondents: the Carnegie 2019 frame held; the catalog is now legible; the Rome Statute case continues. For Filipinos under 45 (the Carnegie cohort): this is the institutional memory you did not get from lived experience. Now you have it.

Twenty-five institutions. Six years.
One catalog. One vote. One deadline.
