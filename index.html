Duterte 2028?
Two reckonings on the drug war before you vote.

Before any Filipino casts a Duterte 2028 ballot — for Sara Duterte, for any of the Duterte clan, or for any candidate selling the same drug-war rhetoric — this is the evidence record. Two single-page explainers on Rodrigo Duterte's drug war: the AMLC findings, the ICC charges, ~30,000 mostly-poor dead, and the composite lie that drug addicts are rapists, killers, and thieves who deserve to die. Built on high-quality sources.

For the brothers, sisters, aunties, uncles, and pastors who still defend the drug war — or quietly tolerated it. The two decks share one moral resolution: we can share the lament; we cannot share the impunity.

Why "Duterte 2028" deserves a complete reckoning first.

01

The evidence is finally on the public record.

The AMLC has now corroborated the Trillanes affidavit on the alleged drug lord Sammy Uy's ₱181.6 M to the Duterte family. The ICC pre-trial chamber has confirmed all charges against Rodrigo Duterte. The Senate Blue Ribbon Committee ruled Duterte "betrayed public trust" on Pharmally. The House Justice Committee disclosed ₱6.77 B in suspicious transactions through Sara Duterte's accounts. Most of this was not available to Filipino voters in 2016 or 2022. It is available now — before 2028.

02

The composite lie still circulates — and will be sold again.

The same rhetoric that justified ~30,000 EJKs — "drug addicts are criminals, rapists, killers, thieves who deserve to die" — is the rhetoric a Duterte 2028 candidacy will run on. The science (NIDA, WHO, CDC, BJS, OJP, peer-reviewed journals) refutes the lie in every direction. The Filipino voter deserves the science before the ballot.

03

It was always a war on the poor — not on drugs.

Amnesty International titled its 2017 report "If you are poor, you are killed." ICC witness testimony: "Oplan Tokhang had to target the poor because they have no capacity to sue." Tens of thousands died in Caloocan alleys, Tondo backlots, Payatas slopes — none from Forbes Park, BGC, or Ayala Heights. A Duterte 2028 vote is a vote to authorize that targeting pattern all over again.

⚠
A note before you open either deck. Both explainers discuss sexual violence, homicide, extrajudicial killings, and the deaths of named individuals. The discussion is general — not just about the drug war — and the content may stir hard memories for any reader who has survived sexual assault, lost a loved one to violence, or carries unresolved grief or anger from a crime, whether recent or from years ago, related to the drug war or not. The work is meant to honor survivors and the dead, not to flatten them into statistics. Please read at your own pace. Each deck closes with support resources — crisis hotlines, legal-aid groups, and survivor-support organizations in the Philippines.
